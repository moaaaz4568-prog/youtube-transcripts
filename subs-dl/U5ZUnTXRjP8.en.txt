My dad was gone before I was born. Then
mom died. I left my sister to take care
of me. My big sister was my everything.
She did it all. Got me to school, fed
me, and helped me do my homework. I
didn't have much, but having her felt
like enough. Then he showed up, and
everything seemed to get better. He was
good to her and he was good to me too.
Took me to school in the mornings and
picked me up afterwards. He felt like
family. But then things start to change.
He took me to places I didn't feel
right. He had me around some shady
people. He said if I really cared about
my sister, I'd help with money. He said
to help, I had to do something. Sleep
with his
friends. I said no. And he hit me.
called me ungrateful, told me to grow
up. I was
11. For two years, he had buyers pick me
up from the library, do their thing, and
drop me back off to him. I didn't know
what to do or how to make it stop.
I saw a sign on the bathroom stall on
how to get help. But I didn't want to be
a burden on my
sister until one day she saw my bruises
and asked me what happened. I finally
told her, terrified that I was going to
disappoint her. But it turns out that he
was doing the same thing to her,
threatening to hurt me if she didn't do
what he said.
I remember the sign from the
bathroom. When I called, Safe House
Project answered. I told the advocate
what happened. She believed me. She
found a safe house in an estate where we
can go together. She kept checking on us
while we wait on the right time to get
away. One day, while he was taking a
shower, my sister grabbed my hand and
Safe House had a Uber outside waiting
for us. They bought our plane tickets
and stayed on the phone every step of
the
way. In a safe house, I finally felt
safe. And over time, I felt hope. My
therapist helped me heal and process
everything I went through. I got to feel
like a kid again and experience
everything I missed out on. I slept in a
bed where I hadn't been abused.
Once we finished the program, we called
Safe House Project and talked to the
advocate who helped us run away from
him. I got to tell her about the amazing
things I
done. She helped us find our next
program. My sister and I got an
apartment and she started a job she
loves. And a year after we got out, we
got to go back and testify against him
for what he did to
us. Every day we keep moving forward. We
are stronger now and surrounded by
people who care about us. I'm grateful
for the freedom that came that day. The
safe project heard me, made a plan, and
gave me courage to escape.
