Why do I always have this feeling that I'm missing something, whether it's a
course or information, and why do I see this as what's holding
me back? It's a defense mechanism of the brain, meaning that the brain, by its very nature, wants to make things easier for you, to keep you in the least painful and uncomfortable situation. So, naturally, any tiring action is detected by the brain as tiring, and it starts to find ways to avoid it. You can't just tell it, "Let's
not take responsibility." If your brain
tells you that, you won't go along with it. And it's very clever at
knowing what you're going to go along with, so it can't just
tell you, "Let's rest." No, it tells you, "You're
supposed to do this, but this thing is painful, important, and urgent." Then it tries to
distract you with something
else, saying, "But this is important too, so
let's finish this first, and then we'll move on to that."
It takes all your focus and puts it on something else
because it's easier. That's how it works.  I
trusted you, so your brain did its job. It's required to
protect you—protect you from fear,
from pain, from boredom [laughs],
from anything bad. It always wants you to be
okay. If something makes you uncomfortable, like
fidgeting, moving around, or jumping, your brain will activate an
emergency mechanism to figure out how to protect it from whatever might hurt it. This is the defense
mechanism, and
its result is procrastination.
Procrastination is simply a result; it's the outcome of the
psychological process happening in your brain without you
realizing it, so your brain can protect itself from the "pen" (a metaphor for something that
might actually be useful at times).
