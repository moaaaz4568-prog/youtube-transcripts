I'm Kamal, a new manager, and these are five things I learned
in my first year of management. The first thing I learned is that
management isn't just about giving orders.
To be a successful manager, you have to be a successful leader.
There's a saying that "lead is serve,"
meaning if you want to lead people, you have to serve them.
Serving them here means helping them
to be their best selves, helping them solve
their problems. This leads us to the second point:
now that you're a manager, you can't work on every
task. You can't work on everything. You
have to learn something called delegation, which means
giving tasks to the right people. Pay attention:
I said delegation, not application. The difference is that
in delegation, you show the person
exactly what you want them to do. You give them a
process, you give them documents, you give them tips to complete the
work. But application means giving
the person the entire task without giving them any
tips. It's like saying, "This is the task, do it."
The third thing I learned is that you
manage people, not robots. Everything
takes time, so you can't give
your team a difficult task at the end of the day and demand it be done
immediately. This leads us to the fourth point: a
good manager listens to people's problems and motivates them
during difficult times when they're exhausted. The fifth
thing I learned in management is that good deeds are private, and so are bad ones.
If a
team member does something excellent, you must praise them
personally and then publicly. If
something bad happens, you must find the source of the
problem, not just hold them accountable, but
