Many people see freelancing as a dream.   Wake up whenever you like. Work from   wherever you like. Choose the clients   you want to deal with. And at the end   of the month, get paid in dollars. But   the truth is, freelancing can be the   best job in the world. And it can be   the worst job in the world. Because   here, you are not just the designer,   the media buyer, or the marketer. You   wear all the hats. You are your own HR.   You are your own sales team. You are   your own management. You are your own   project manager. Meaning, instead of   being an employee at one company. You   might find yourself an employee for 10   clients at the same time. So, why could   freelancing be the best job in the   world? We will talk about the   advantages of freelancing now, and then   clarify the downsides. First, freedom:   the best thing about freelancing is   that you aren't tied to a specific   place. Work from home, work from a cafe  , work while traveling, or work from   outside the country entirely. But   beware, freedom here doesn't mean you   won't work. Or that you will work very   little and earn a lot. Freedom means   you are the one who chooses your way of   working. You are the one who chooses   your working hours. You are the one who   chooses the client that fits your work,   and this is a huge advantage for anyone   who likes to control their day and   dislikes traditional routines. Second,   you have no boss over your head. You   have no one asking you what you are   doing, what you will do, and what you   are supposed to do. You are the one who   chooses how you work. You are the one   who organizes your day. You are the one   who chooses when to work and when to   rest. You are the one who builds the   system that suits you. This is a great   advantage for anyone who loves the idea   of independence. But keep in mind, the   idea of having no boss doesn't mean you   have no responsibility. Often, being   your own boss is much harder than   having a boss. Because no one will wake   you up. No one will chase after you. No   one will double-check your work. No one   will chase you for deadlines. No one   will ask you when the delivery is or   where the work is, except the client.   And then you might be late and   everything falls apart. So the   advantage here isn't that you don't   have a boss. The advantage here is that   you are fully responsible for yourself.   Third, your income is not tied to a   fixed salary. In a job, your income has   a ceiling. The raise has a limit and is   predetermined. A promotion might come,   or it might not. But in freelancing,   your income can increase quickly. If   you know how to price yourself. If you   know how to market your work. If you   know how to get clients and build a   reputation in the market. Meaning, for   the same skill you get paid 'X' for at   a company, you could work with three or   four clients and double that price. But   here you must know something very, very  , very important: freelancing doesn't   reward the skilled. Freelancing rewards   those who know how to sell their skills  . Fourth, you learn quickly in   freelancing. You deal with different   clients. You deal in different markets.   You deal with different industries and   different problems. One day you work   for a food brand, another a clinic,   then e-commerce, then courses. Unlike   working in a company or in-house, where   things are a bit limited. And if   in-house, you are working in just one   field. You are always learning from the   client's nature, from rejection, from   revisions. Everything gives you   experience in a specific area. The last   advantage is that you build your name.   Freelancing gives you a chance, not   just to build your name inside a   company. It gives you a chance to build   your name in the market itself. Every   good project you. Worked on is a real   case study. Every satisfied client   increases your value. And increases   your opportunities in the market over   time. People aren't just looking for   any designer. People are looking   specifically for you. And this is   something very powerful. For you to   transform from someone looking for work  . To someone whom the work is looking   for. We've clarified the advantages of   freelancing. So you see the world as   rosy right now. Let me shock you and   tell you why freelancing could be the   worst job in the world. First thing:   there is no stable security. If you   work in a company, you come in knowing   and sure that at the end of the month,   I have a specific salary coming in. So   I can rely on installments, rely on   savings circles, especially for people   with many obligations and those   supporting families. Whereas in   freelancing, one month you might earn a   lot. Maybe another month nothing at all  . Maybe a month is slow. So freelancing   is never suitable for disorganized   people. Those who don't have a   financial plan and don't know how to   manage their money. So you have to   calculate your months. You must have a   reserve. You must know that just   because I earned a lot this month, I   don't have to spend all that money.   Second thing: the client doesn't always   understand. One of the hardest parts of   freelancing is dealing with the client   directly. And sometimes, or very often,   clients don't understand. They think   that a logo is something done just like   that. So they tell you, for example, "  make the logo bigger." The design that   takes you eight hours might be finished   in 10 minutes. "Why are revisions so   difficult? Let me revise at my leisure   until we reach something good." So you   must know that a big part of   freelancing is not just execution. A   big part of it is education,   expectation management, and being able   to explain the value of the work.   Because a smart freelancer is always   the one able to protect their time and   effort. Third thing: you are   responsible for everything in the   company. You get a specific task and   finish it. If a problem happens, you   can talk to the Art Director, the   management, or finance. There is   someone to refer back to. But in   freelancing, you are all of those. You   are the one who finds the client. You   are the one who sends payment details.   You are the one who collects the money.   You are the one responsible if there   are revisions. You are the one if. A   misunderstanding happens, you handle   the situation. And that makes many   people eventually hate freelancing. Not   because the work is bad. But because   the management is truly exhausting. The   last thing is isolation. Freelancing   keeps you alone a lot. There’s no   team around you. No one to discuss   things back and forth with. No one to   review your work. No friends around you  . So being alone like that can be very   bad sometimes. Sometimes it can lead   you to a state of depression. And I   know many people who reached that point  . So you must have a community. You   must have friends in your same field.   Go out and meet up. Discuss what   problems you’ve faced. Brainstorm   together. Each one shares their own   case study. The important thing is not   to let yourself just sit in your room.   And sit at your device all the time.   There’s a big illusion people have,   saying "I’ll quit my job to earn more   and relax more." But the truth is, in   the beginning, you’ll work harder   than an employee. And you’ll be more   stressed than an employee. And you   might even earn less. But the   difference here is that you’re   building something for yourself.   Something for your name that will   increase in value over time. So   freelancing at first is not freedom at   all. Freelancing is training for you to   become a business. Not just someone who   executes tasks and that’s it. So when   is freelancing right for you? If you   see that these things are present in   you. Then congratulations to you. You   are capable of freelancing. If   something is missing, you can work on   it until you improve it and become   capable. The first thing is having a   real skill you can sell. There must be   a specific skill you have that is in   demand in the market. And that people   really need. The second thing is   patience in dealing with clients. You   can’t be a short-tempered person in   freelancing at all. You must have long   patience. You must be able to handle   the client. And many times, you need to   soothe the client and handle them with   care. The third thing is being able to   organize your time. There is no manager   over your head. If you walk randomly or   keep things chaotic, everything will   fall apart. Time management is one of   the most important things you must have   if you’re going to freelance. The   fourth thing is to be ready to learn.   Not just execution and technical work.   You must learn pricing. You must learn.   Negotiation. You must learn sales.   Learn communication. Among the most   important things in freelancing are   sales and communication. The fifth   thing is bearing that some months will   come where you are pressured and   crushed. You might stay two or three   days without leaving the house. And   there might be months where things are   very comfortable and the atmosphere is   calm. But if you want freelancing to   escape commitment, then freelancing has   more commitment than a regular job. So,   how do I start? Start correctly. If   I’m someone who has been working for   a long time at a company and I want to   start being independent and building my   name. What steps or process should I   follow? The first thing is you must   start alongside your job. You can’t   just quit your job overnight suddenly.   You must have a steady income from   freelancing so that you can leave your   job feeling reassured. The second thing   is to create a clear portfolio. You   don't need to have 100 projects. Five   quality projects are more than enough.   But it must be clear that you are a   specialist in something. I am a person   who does social media design. I am a   person who does branding. I am a person   who does retouching and photo   manipulation. Specialization in   freelancing always helps you stand out.   The fourth thing is to learn to price   based on value, not just your time. You   must learn to price based on your   valuable experience and the value the   client receives. The size of the client  , the nature of the work, and the   market itself. Is this being done in   Egypt? Is it being done in a foreign   country? Is it being done in a European   country? The last thing is to be able   to write a proposal and a clear   agreement on the number of revisions   and deliverables. Or what you will   deliver and what the files will look   like? Will you deliver images or open   source files? If you are delivering   images and they need open source files,   what are the fees? How will the payment   work? What is the deposit and what   happens after we finish? Or what is the   payment process or the payment plan?   All these things must be clear from the   beginning. Now, let's move to a very   important question: at the start of my   career, is it better to work in a   company or start freelancing right away  ? If you are at the beginning of your   career, my advice to you is to work in   a company, even for a short period. Why  ? Because a company will teach you   things you won't easily learn in   freelancing. You will learn what a   brief is. You will learn what a   deadline is. You will learn how to deal   with a team. You will learn how to work   under pressure. You will learn what   client feedback is. And how to take   feedback and handle it. What it means   for your work to be part of a campaign.   What it means to apply for a pitch.   What it means to have a manager   reviewing your work. Or an art director   above you reviewing your work. In   freelancing, at the start, you will   mostly be working alone. So, there is   no one to fix your mistakes.   Consequently, your development will be   very slow. A company gives you exposure   and interaction. And exposure at the   beginning is much more important than   money. Because you see the market from   the inside. You understand how the   client speaks. You understand how to   deliver your files correctly. You   understand—and this is a very   important point—that design is not   just something that looks nice. It is   something that achieves a specific   marketing goal. You learn that work is   not just about your mood. Work is   something that achieves a goal.   Something set for a specific budget.   Something that needs to be finished in   a specific time. But everything I am   telling you doesn't mean you must be   100%ready to start freelancing. So,   what is the solution? The solution is   to do both together smartly. Work in a   company to learn the system, speed, and   interaction. And at the same time, do   some small freelance work on the side.   To build a portfolio and experience   dealing with clients. I always say that   the company will teach you the craft.   But freelancing will teach you the   market. And keep in mind, if you start   freelancing without trying a company,   it doesn't mean you will fail. You   might succeed. But the road will be   much harder. Because here, you will   learn by trial and error. And often,   trial and error costs you more money.   It costs you more time. And it will   surely cost you clients in the   beginning. The last thing I want to   tell you is that freelancing is the   first door, not the end of the road.   You start as a freelancer. Then you   build a strong personal brand. You   might then be able to build a studio.   Followed by a small agency. Then you   become able to create a full business.   So freelancing isn't just a job. It can   be the beginning of creating a whole   entity. But the condition here, and the   most important thing, is that you   shouldn't keep selling your time. Over   time, you must start building systems.   Pricing methods. Communication methods.   Offers. Case studies. Work processes.   And after a while, you could have a   team to help you. In the end, I want to   tell you that if you're entering   freelancing to escape your boss, you   will struggle. And if you're entering   freelancing for your name, income, and   freedom, you will also struggle. But   your struggle here has meaning.   Ultimately, the question isn't whether   freelancing is good or bad. The real   question is: are you ready to manage   yourself like a company?
