Many people might strive very hard on a path
that doesn't align with their divine gift, so you'll find them
constantly consoling themselves with the thought, "God will surely
reward me," even though they're not happy doing something but
forcing themselves. You see this example often. The point is, for
you, the reward should be like
your favorite game [clears throat]—the reward is
n't the score you get at the end.
You're playing your game, and the score doesn't matter as
much as the enjoyment of the activity itself. It doesn't matter what
happens—whether it's
accepted or not, whether it brings good fortune to others or not, whether it
backfires or not. None of that matters. What matters is that
when you find
yourself doing something, it becomes the reward itself. You don't
feel like you've sacrificed something and need to compensate. This is very, very important—to get your
due
enjoyment from the action itself, so that even before the
result comes, you feel you've already received it.  I've earned my living and found
joy in doing what I
love. At this point, you'll find immense psychological energy that
allows you to persevere no matter what happens. It might
surprise people that you're continuing, as if it doesn't give you
anything they see, like, "
Why are you doing this, girl?" The
whole point is, who can do this?
Who truly enjoys the process?
